Critical Illness Insurance

A lump sum right when you need it most, so a serious diagnosis doesn't also become a financial problem.

Who this insurance is for

Families with fixed commitments

If you have a mortgage, rent or children to support, the bills keep coming even when everything at home has come to a stop. Having cash available gives you room to get organised without rushing.

Self-employed people and professionals

When your income depends on you being able to work, a long illness affects your health and your business at the same time. It's worth having thought in advance about how to cover that gap.

Anyone who already has life insurance

Many life policies can include a critical illness capital advance. We review yours to find out whether you have it, on what terms, and whether it suits you.

What to consider before taking out cover

The specific cover, the illnesses included and their limits depend on the insurer and the option you choose. At Retuerto y Asociados we put them in writing before you decide.

Critical illness capital advance

This is one of the standard covers on our life insurance. What proportion of the capital is advanced, and under what circumstances, is set by each policy.

Which illnesses the policy covers

Check which illnesses are included and, above all, how they're defined. There's no list common to all insurers: what counts is what's written in your terms.

Waiting period

Ask from when the cover applies. It's common for there to be a waiting period, and its length depends on the insurer: ask for it in writing.

Additional or advanced capital

Check whether what you receive on diagnosis is deducted from the death benefit or paid on top of it. It makes a big difference to what's left afterwards for your family.

Health questionnaire and exclusions

Ask how the insurer treats pre-existing conditions and what exclusions apply. Answering the questionnaire accurately avoids problems on the day you need to make a claim.

Ages, duration and requirements to claim

Ask up to what age you can take out and keep the cover, what medical documentation is required for payment, and whether there's any deadline or condition after the diagnosis.

How it works

The idea is simple: if you're diagnosed with one of the illnesses the policy defines as critical, the insurer pays you a lump sum. It doesn't reimburse medical bills or give you access to a network of specialists; it hands you money to use as you need, whether to cover household bills while you can't work, to adapt your home, or to pay for travel and care.

On our website, this cover appears as a critical illness capital advance within life insurance: instead of waiting until death, part of the capital taken out can be paid while you're alive when the diagnosis occurs. There are also products on the market where critical illness cover is taken out separately or as an add-on. We explain what each insurer offers and how it would be reflected in your policy.

When the time comes, the process involves reporting the diagnosis to the insurer and providing whatever medical reports it requests. At Retuerto y Asociados we handle that process with you, from gathering the documentation to following the claim through to payment.

What's worth knowing

It doesn't replace health insurance. Health insurance gives you access to private healthcare (specialists, diagnostic tests, hospitalisation and a second medical opinion); the critical illness cover gives you liquidity. These are different needs, and many families end up combining both.

It's also not the same as accident insurance, which only responds when the harm is caused by an accident, nor the same as our special long-term care insurance, which pays an income or a lump sum when severe or major dependency is recognised, whether it results from an accident or an illness.

The small print matters here more than in almost any other type of insurance. Two policies can call an illness by the same name and define it differently, with different requirements for payment. That's why we ask you not to compare on price alone: we compare the definitions, the waiting period and the exclusions in writing before you choose.

What to weigh up before taking out cover

Start from your own figures: how much your family would need to keep up with fixed expenses for a few months without your income, what savings you have, and what other policies already protect you. That's what should decide the sum insured, not a round figure. If you already have life insurance, for example one linked to your mortgage, bring it in: it may already include this cover, or it may be worth topping it up. On your state of health, your treatment or your medical decisions, your doctor is always the reference point; we take care of the insurance side.

Frequently asked questions

Which illnesses does it cover?

Whichever the policy expressly defines, and each insurer has its own list and its own definitions. There's no list common to the whole market, so we show you in writing the one for each option we propose.

Do I have to stop working to be paid?

In most approaches, what triggers payment is the diagnosis of an included illness, not being signed off work. Even so, the specific requirements are set by each policy, and we go through them with you before you take out cover.

How is it different from my health insurance?

Health insurance gives you private medical care; this cover pays you a lump sum you can use freely. One takes care of your medical treatment and the other of your finances, which is why they complement each other.

If I claim the advance, does my family lose the life cover?

It depends on how the policy is structured. If it's an advance, what you receive is usually deducted from the death benefit your beneficiaries would later receive; if it's additional cover, it isn't. It's one of the first things we check.

Can I take it out if I've already had an illness?

It depends on the insurer and on what you declare in the health questionnaire. It may be accepted with exclusions, on different terms, or not accepted at all. Tell us about your case and we'll check with several insurers on your behalf.

From when am I covered?

From the end of whatever waiting period the policy sets, if there is one. Its length varies by insurer, which is why we set it out in writing in the comparison.

Mistaken beliefs

What people tend to believe, and what actually happens

Five ideas we often hear that turn out to be costly on the day there's a claim.

"Going it alone is cheaper for me."

The broker costs you nothing: the insurer pays them. And we compare several insurers at once that you'd otherwise have to approach one by one.

"All policies are the same."

Two policies at the same price can have very different limits, excesses and exclusions. What matters is what the terms say on the day of the claim.

"The broker works for the insurer."

The broker represents the insured party and their interests. It's the insurer's agent who represents the company, and that's a different role altogether.

"If I have a claim, I'll sort it out myself."

How the claim is opened and documented shapes everything that follows. We handle it, and the claim process too.

"Switching insurer is a hassle."

We handle it for you, including cancelling the previous policy in time so it doesn't renew on its own.

"The compulsory cover is enough for me."

The legal minimum covers what you cause to third parties, not what happens to you. That's the difference between a scare and a real problem.
