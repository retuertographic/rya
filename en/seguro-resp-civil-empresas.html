Liability Insurance

General, employer's, product and completed-operations liability, tailored to the business's sector and turnover.

What does it cover?

Liability cover includes payment of compensation for bodily injury, material damage or financial loss caused to third parties for which the insured party, or those they are responsible for, may be at fault, arising from events in their private or professional life.

It includes, among others, damage to third parties caused by water, fire or explosion, and damage caused by the acts or omissions of domestic staff.

For a business, liability cover is sized according to the sector, turnover and geographic scope of the activity, and is coordinated with the rest of the insurance programme so there are no gaps or overlaps.

Typical cover

  • Operational liability
  • Employer's liability
  • Premises liability
  • Product and completed-operations liability
  • Bodily injury, material damage and financial loss to third parties
  • Legal defence and provision of bonds

Common misconceptions

What people tend to believe, and what actually happens

Five ideas we hear often that prove costly the day there's a claim.

"Going it alone is cheaper."

The broker costs you nothing: the insurer pays them. And they compare several companies at once that you'd otherwise have to approach one by one.

"All policies are the same."

Two policies at the same price can have very different limits, excesses and exclusions. What matters is what the terms say on the day of the claim.

"The broker works for the insurer."

The broker represents the insured party and their interests. It's the insurer's agent who represents the company — a different role altogether.

"If I have a claim, I'll sort it out myself."

How the claim report is opened and documented shapes everything that follows. We handle it, and the claim itself too.

"Switching insurer is a hassle."

We handle it for you, including cancelling the previous policy in time so it doesn't renew automatically.

"The compulsory cover is enough for me."

The legal minimum covers what you cause to others, not what happens to you. That's the difference between a scare and a real problem.
