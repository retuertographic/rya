Directors and Officers (D&O)

Protects the personal assets of those who manage or direct the company against claims arising from their management decisions.

Why take out D&O cover

You answer with your own assets

A director is personally and unlimitedly liable for damage caused by their management decisions. The company has limited liability; the person running it does not.

Defending yourself costs money

Even if the claim is ultimately dismissed, the lawyer, court representative and expert reports have to be paid for from day one. The policy advances these costs.

The role doesn't expire

Claims can be made over decisions taken years earlier, even after leaving the role. The cover is designed precisely for that.

What it covers

The specific cover and its limits depend on the company and the option you take out. At Retuerto y Asociados we set them out in writing before you decide.

Legal expenses

Lawyer, court representative and expert costs from the moment the claim is notified, without waiting for the outcome of proceedings.

Compensation

The amounts the insured party must pay as the party liable for their management decisions, up to the limit taken out.

Bonds

Setting up the civil bonds a court may require to secure the outcome of proceedings.

Claims from the company and shareholders

Corporate and individual liability actions brought by the company itself, by its shareholders, or by affected third parties.

Administrative proceedings

Assistance during inspections and proceedings brought by public bodies. It does not cover the amount of fines, which are personal and non-transferable.

Insolvency-related liability

Defence against actions arising from the classification of insolvency proceedings and from claims for company debts.

Who is covered

Both formally appointed and de facto directors, managers with executive functions and, depending on the policy, their heirs and spouse.

Cover after leaving the role

An additional period to notify claims that arrive after leaving the post or after the policy ends.

Like any liability policy, it does not cover wilful misconduct or unlawful gain by the insured party: if a final court ruling establishes this, the insurer will claim back what it advanced.

Why the company's liability cover isn't enough

The company's general liability policy covers damage the business's activity causes to third parties: a customer who slips, a defective product, poorly executed work. The company is liable.

D&O covers something different: a claim brought personally against the decision-maker. It's not a claim over physical damage, but over a management decision — a loss-making deal, a neglected tax obligation, poorly handled insolvency — and the person is asked to answer with their own assets.

That's why the two policies exist side by side. One protects the company's coffers; the other, the director's home.

Who should have it

It isn't compulsory, but there are situations where going without it is hard to justify: companies with shareholders who take no part in day-to-day management, businesses with bank financing or investors, boards with external directors, and any company in a regulated sector.

The same goes for small family businesses, where the director is usually the one with the most personal assets at stake and where disputes between partners more often than people think end up in a liability claim.

At Retuerto y Asociados we size the limit to match the balance sheet, the corporate structure and the sector, and we scrutinise the exclusions closely, since that's where this product is really decided.

Frequently asked questions

Does the company pay for it, or the director?

Usually the company pays the premium, even though the people insured are the individuals who manage and direct it. It's an accepted and common arrangement.

Does it cover fines and penalties?

No. Administrative penalties are personal and no policy can cover them. What it does cover is the defence and assistance during proceedings.

What if I leave the role?

Claims can be brought over earlier decisions for years afterwards. That's why these policies include a subsequent notification period; we always check what each company offers before recommending it.

Does it cover fraud by the director themselves?

No. Wilful misconduct and unlawful gain are excluded. The insurer may advance the defence costs, but if a final ruling establishes fraudulent conduct, it will claim back what it paid.

Common misconceptions

What people tend to believe, and what actually happens

Five ideas we hear often, and that end up costing dearly on the day of a claim.

"Going it alone works out cheaper."

A broker costs you nothing: the insurer pays them. And a broker compares several companies at once that you would otherwise have to approach one by one.

"All policies are the same."

Two policies at the same price can have very different limits, excesses and exclusions. What matters is what the terms say on the day of the claim.

"The broker works for the insurer."

The broker represents the insured party and their interests. The person who represents the company is its agent, which is a different role altogether.

"If I have a claim, I'll sort it out myself."

How the report is opened and documented shapes everything that follows. We handle it for you, and the claim itself too.

"Switching insurer is a hassle."

We handle it for you, including cancelling the previous policy in time so it doesn't renew automatically.

"The compulsory minimum is enough for me."

The legal minimum covers what you cause to others, not what happens to you. That's the difference between a scare and a real problem.
