    Common misconceptions

    What people tend to believe, and what actually happens

    Five ideas we often hear that turn out to be costly on the day something goes wrong.

    "Going it alone is cheaper."

    A broker costs you nothing: they're paid by the insurer. And they compare several insurers at once that you'd otherwise have to approach one by one.

    "All policies are the same."

    Two policies at the same price can have very different limits, excesses and exclusions. What matters is what the terms say on the day of the claim.

    "The broker works for the insurer."

    The broker represents the insured party and their interests. It's the agent, a different role entirely, who represents the insurance company.

    "If something happens, I'll sort it out myself."

    How the claim is opened and documented shapes everything that follows. We handle it, and the claim itself, for you.

    "Switching insurer is a hassle."

    We handle it for you, including cancelling the previous policy in time so it doesn't renew automatically.

    "The compulsory cover is enough for me."

    The legal minimum covers what you cause to others, not what happens to you. That's the difference between a scare and a real problem.
