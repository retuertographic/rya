What you should know about

What an Insurance Broker really is

A broker is the expert who mediates between the insurance company and the client, representing the insured party and their interests, rather than the insurer alone.

We are the only professionals legally qualified to advise with strict independence and impartiality with regard to insurance companies.

About us

Common misconceptions

What people tend to believe, and what actually happens

Five ideas we hear often, and that prove costly the day there's a claim.

"Going it alone is cheaper."

The broker costs you nothing: they're paid by the insurer. And they compare several companies at once, which you'd otherwise have to approach one by one.

"All policies are the same."

Two policies at the same price can have very different limits, excesses and exclusions. What matters is what the terms say on the day of the claim.

"The broker works for the insurer."

The broker represents the insured party and their interests. It's the agent who represents the company, and that's a different role altogether.

"If I have a claim, I'll sort it out myself."

How the claim is opened and documented shapes the whole process that follows. We handle it, and the claim itself too.

"Switching insurer is a hassle."

We handle it, including cancelling the previous policy in time so it doesn't renew automatically.

"The compulsory insurance is enough for me."

The legal minimum covers what you cause to others, not what happens to you. That's the difference between a scare and a real problem.
