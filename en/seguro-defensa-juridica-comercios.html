Legal Expenses Insurance

Defence in employment, contractual and administrative claims.

What does it cover?

Legal expenses insurance provides for the defence of the insured party's interests, advances any compensation that may be due to the policyholder, and provides telephone legal assistance.

The insurer first attempts an amicable settlement and only then turns to legal proceedings, where the insured suffers a loss that could give rise to a claim against a third party. This includes legal advice from expert solicitors.

Typical cover

  • Defence of the insured party's interests
  • Claims for damage against third parties
  • Advance of compensation
  • Telephone legal assistance
  • Amicable settlement before legal proceedings
  • Solicitor and court agent fees

Common misconceptions

What people tend to believe, and what really happens

Five ideas we hear often, which prove costly on the day there's a claim.

"Going it alone is cheaper."

A broker costs you nothing: the insurer pays them. And we compare several companies at once, which you would otherwise have to approach one by one.

"All policies are the same."

Two policies at the same price can have very different limits, excesses and exclusions. What matters is what the terms say on the day of the claim.

"The broker works for the insurer."

A broker represents the insured party and their interests. The person who represents the company is its agent, a different role altogether.

"If I have a claim, I'll sort it out myself."

How the report is opened and documented shapes everything that follows. We handle it, and the claim itself too.

"Switching insurer is a hassle."

We handle it for you, including cancelling the previous policy in time so it doesn't renew on its own.

"The compulsory minimum is enough for me."

The legal minimum covers what you cause to others, not what happens to you. That's the difference between a scare and a real problem.
